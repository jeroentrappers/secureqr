Privacy policy

Last updated: 12 November 2026

SecureQR is a QR code scanner for iPhone and Android, published by Appmire CommV (Belgium, company number BE 0535.825.723). This page says what the app does with your data. Questions: privacy@appmire.be.

In short

  • The app has no account and no advertising, and we run no analytics or tracking of our own.
  • Only web links are sent to our server, so it can visit the link for you. Everything else in a QR code is read on your phone and stays there.
  • Camera images never leave your phone and are not stored.
  • Your scan history is kept on your phone only.
  • We do not sell or share your data, and we do not use it to build a profile of you.

What stays on your phone

  • The camera. The app uses the camera to find and read QR codes. The picture is processed on the phone, in memory, and is not saved or sent anywhere. If you pick a picture from your photo library instead, only that picture is read, on the phone.
  • Codes that are not web links. Bank transfers, payment requests, Wi-Fi networks, contacts, calendar events, sign-in codes, phone numbers, text and links that open another app are read on the phone and never sent to us.
  • Scan history. The list of what you scanned is stored in the app's private storage on your phone. You can delete single items or clear the whole list in the app, and it is removed when you uninstall the app. On Android it is left out of cloud backups. On iPhone it is part of your own iCloud or computer backup of the phone, if you use one.

What is sent to our server

When a QR code contains a web link (it starts with http:// or https://), the app sends that link to our server at qr.appmire.be over an encrypted connection. Nothing else is sent with it: no name, no account, no device or advertising identifier, no location. As with any internet connection, our server sees the IP address the request comes from.

The server opens the link in a browser of its own, follows the redirects, takes a picture of the page it ends on and sends the result back to the app. The website behind the link sees a visit from our server, not from your phone.

Others involved

  • Blocklist checks. To see whether a site is on a threat list, our server looks up the site names on the link's path (not the full link) at Quad9, Cloudflare and DNS4EU, as ordinary DNS lookups. These services see our server's address, not yours. A further list of phishing sites is downloaded to our server and checked there.
  • The QR reader on Android. The Android app reads QR codes with Google's ML Kit library, on the phone. Camera pictures and the contents of a code are not sent to Google. The library does send Google technical data about its own use: device and app information, performance figures, error codes and an identifier for the installation that does not identify you or your phone. Google describes this in its ML Kit terms. The iPhone app uses Apple's built-in reader and sends nothing of this kind.
  • Hosting. The server is rented from Hetzner Online GmbH and is located in Germany.
  • The site behind the link. Checking a link means visiting it. That site receives the link, from our server.

We do not pass your data to anyone else, unless the law requires it.

Permissions

  • Camera: to read QR codes. You can refuse and pick a picture instead.
  • Internet: to ask our server to check a web link.

Why we may process this

Under the GDPR we rely on our legitimate interest in providing the check you ask for and in keeping the service secure (article 6(1)(f)). We process as little as possible to do that.

Your rights

You can ask us what data we hold about you, and ask us to correct or delete it or to stop processing it. Because we have no accounts, the only thing we could look up is an IP address in the logs described above. Write to privacy@appmire.be. You can also complain to the Belgian Data Protection Authority (dataprotectionauthority.be) or the authority in your own country.

Children

The app is not aimed at children and does not knowingly collect data from them.

Changes

If the app or the service changes in a way that affects this policy, we update this page and the date at the top.
